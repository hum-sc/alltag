# Thoughts on the Modern Web

I've been thinking a lot lately about how different the web feels compared to ten years ago.

## The Weight of Modern Sites

Open the browser's network tab on a typical news article. You'll see dozens of requests, megabytes of JavaScript, and half a dozen tracking pixels.

Compare that to a page from 1999:

- Pure HTML
- Maybe a small GIF or two
- A &lt;table&gt; for layout
- Total size: under 50KB

Those pages loaded in under a second on a dial-up modem. Today's pages can struggle on broadband.

## What We Gained

I'm not entirely nostalgic. Modern tooling has given us:

- Accessible web apps
- Real-time collaboration
- Responsive design

## What We Lost

But somewhere along the way we also lost:

- The ability to `view-source` and understand a page
- Reasonable page weights
- Pages that work without JavaScript

## A Middle Ground

Maybe the answer isn't to go back, but to be more *intentional*. Not every site needs a full React app. A blog — like this one — works perfectly well as static HTML.
